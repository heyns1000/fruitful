Global Brand Standards · Part 1 — Core Identity
Block Box
The non-negotiable foundation every later chapter inherits. The 10c identity system is fixed and final — this chapter governs correct application, not design intent.
01 — Logo System
The mark, its lockups, and its boundaries
Primary mark
Small mark (favicon/stamp)
Horizontal lockup
Block Box
Stacked lockup
Clearspace, minimum size & version selection
Purpose: protect the mark's legibility and prevent crowding from copy, other logos, or edge cropping.
Core standard: clearspace on all sides equals the full height of the tear-wave cue, measured from the outer edge of the rounded block. No text, image, edge, or competing mark may enter this zone.
Minimums/maximums: print 12mm width floor (small mark), 18mm width floor (primary/horizontal lockup); digital 24px width floor (small mark), 32px floor (primary/horizontal lockup). No enforced maximum, but any use above 400mm print / 400px digital must be reviewed for image quality, not redrawn.
Version selection: primary mark for hero packaging/print contexts ≥18mm; horizontal lockup for single-line contexts (shelf strips, headers, email); stacked lockup for tall/narrow contexts (side panels, mobile nav); small mark (no wordmark) below the wordmark floor or at favicon/app-icon/stamp scale; one-color mark for single-ink print, embossing, engraving, or thermal label runs.
Incorrect use: using the primary mark with wordmark below 18mm/32px; substituting the stacked lockup where the horizontal lockup is specified for single-line contexts.
Background, contrast & reproduction on difficult surfaces
Purpose: guarantee the mark stays legible regardless of substrate.
Core standard: mark must sit on Deep Navy, Value Yellow, Warm Paper, or true white only. Minimum contrast ratio between mark and background is 4.5:1.
Approved variants: full-color mark on Warm Paper/white; reversed (yellow-on-navy) mark on Deep Navy; one-color navy or yellow mark for foil, emboss, laser-etch, or corrugate print where full color is not production-viable.
Forbidden: placing the mark on photography without a solid holding shape behind it; placing it on any brand color outside the four approved backgrounds; placing it on textured corrugate without a printed white/yellow field beneath.
Global-use consideration: for low-cost offset or single-ink runs common in some manufacturing regions, the one-color mark is mandatory, not optional — full-color reproduction is never assumed available.
Prohibited manipulations
No stretching or disproportionate scaling. No rotation beyond an approved 18° tilt used only in the packaging tear-icon motif, never on the primary lockup. No recoloring outside the four-color system defined in 1.2. No drop shadows, glows, bevels, or outlines added to the mark. No redrawing the tear-wave as a literal jagged cut, dotted perforation line, or scissors icon — it must remain the single soft continuous wave. No cropping the rounded block into a square or sharp-cornered shape. No combining the mark with unapproved taglines baked into the lockup itself.
Co-existence with product names & SKUs
Core standard: product/profile identifiers (e.g. "NT108 · 110mm") are always set in Archivo, never Fredoka, and sit below or beside the lockup — never inside its clearspace, never sharing its type style. SKU codes and barcodes belong on the back panel only, at minimum 1x clearspace distance from the mark.
Favicon & app-icon behavior
Core standard: at icon scale (≤64px) the small mark is used full-bleed to the icon's edge with no clearspace padding — the icon container itself provides the margin. Navy background, yellow rounded block, yellow tear-wave stroke. Never the reversed (yellow background) version at this scale — it loses contrast against most OS icon trays.
02 — Color System
Palette & usage proportions
Deep Navy
#0E2148 · RGB 14,33,72 · CMYK 90,66,20,60
Value Yellow
#F5C518 · RGB 245,197,24 · CMYK 4,20,95,0
Tear Orange
#E8622C · RGB 232,98,44 · CMYK 0,66,86,0
Warm Paper (neutral)
#EEE6D6 · RGB 238,230,214
Roles: Deep Navy and Value Yellow are structural — they carry the mark itself and are never optional. Tear Orange is accent-only, reserved for the tear-wave detail and small callouts; it must never fill a large area or replace Navy/Yellow as a dominant field. Warm Paper is the neutral background for print collateral and documents — never used on the mark itself.

Usage ratios: Packaging fronts — Navy ~50-60%, Yellow ~25-30% (block fill + Value Guarantee label), Orange ≤10%. Digital UI — Navy for headers/nav, Yellow reserved for CTAs and the mark, Orange for hover/active states only. POS/signage — Yellow dominant for shelf-distance visibility, Navy for text and mark. Technical/spec documents — Warm Paper background, Navy text, color used only in diagrams.

One-color, low-ink & B/W fallback: when only one ink is available, use solid Navy (preferred) or solid Yellow. When printing is grayscale-only, render the mark in 100% black on white or reversed white-on-black — never a gray tint, which fails at small sizes and low-cost print.
03 — Typography
Two typefaces, clear hierarchy
Fredoka
Primary — wordmark, headlines, packaging front copy. Rounded, warm, consumer-first.
Archivo
Secondary — body copy, spec sheets, web UI, technical documents. Neutral, legible at small size.
Type roles: Brand display (Fredoka Bold) — wordmark and headlines only, never smaller than 24px/18pt. Packaging display (Fredoka SemiBold) — front-panel product name, hero packaging copy. UI/body (Archivo Medium/Regular) — web, app, all running text. Technical/spec text (Archivo Regular, mono figures) — dimensions, tolerances, part numbers. Legal/regulatory text (Archivo Regular, minimum 6pt/8px) — compliance copy, fine print, always Navy on light or white on Navy, never Yellow (fails contrast at small size).

Case & spacing: product name in title case always ("Block Box," never "BLOCKBOX" or "block box"). Labels/kickers (e.g. "VALUE GUARANTEE") in uppercase Archivo Bold with +0.06–0.12em tracking. Body copy sentence case, no forced tracking.

Never allowed: Fredoka for body copy or legal text at any size; Archivo for the wordmark itself; condensed or italic styling of either face; justified body text (ragged-right only).

Multilingual fallback: where Fredoka lacks full glyph coverage for a target language, substitute a rounded geometric sans of equivalent weight for headlines only (e.g. a local rounded-sans matching x-height); body copy always falls back to a standard system sans in that script, keeping Archivo's role logic intact.
01.4 — Tone of Voice
How Block Box speaks to each audience
Principle: warmth and clarity first, technical authority earned second — never the reverse. Voice shifts by audience, not by channel.

Consumers (packaging, retail, web): plain, friendly, benefit-first. Approved: "Snap out exactly what you need — no cutting, no waste." Disallowed: dense spec language or patent terminology on-pack.

Installers/warehouse (spec sheets, training): direct, procedural, respectful of their time. Approved: "Apply 2–5kg force along the marked line. No tools required." Disallowed: marketing adjectives ("revolutionary," "game-changing") in procedural text.

Retailers/licensees (briefs, contracts, sell sheets): confident, numbers-led, commercially precise. Approved: "93% packaging cost reduction, verified in commercial deployment." Disallowed: vague superlatives without a backing figure.

Support/documentation: calm, precise, solution-first. Approved: "If the bridge doesn't separate cleanly, check profile alignment before reapplying force." Disallowed: apologetic or uncertain phrasing that undercuts trust in the product.
Implementation checklist — Part 1 sign-off
☐ Mark used at or above minimum size for its context, correct version selected for orientation/scale
☐ Clearspace respected; no prohibited manipulation applied
☐ Background is one of the four approved fields with 4.5:1 contrast
☐ Colors match approved usage ratios for the channel (packaging / digital / POS / document)
☐ One-color fallback confirmed for any single-ink or grayscale production run
☐ Type roles match the hierarchy — no Fredoka body copy, no Archivo wordmark
☐ Copy tone matches the target audience (consumer / installer / retailer / support)
☐ Any ambiguous case escalated to brand governance (Part 8) rather than improvised

Global Brand Standards · Part 3
In-Store & POS
Shelf strips, wobblers, end-caps, signage — the brand's moment of physical persuasion at point of sale. Parts 1, 2, 5 and 7 are fixed and inherited.
03.1 — Shelf Strips & Rail Tags
The most common in-store touchpoint
Core standard: horizontal lockup, single line, Value Yellow background for maximum shelf-edge visibility, minimum height 40mm per Part 1/7 legibility floor. Rail tags (narrow shelf-edge strips) drop to small mark + product ID only when strip height is below 40mm.

Approved variants: printed card strip (retailer-installed); printed adhesive rail tag (manufacturer-supplied). Both follow identical hierarchy: mark → product ID → price/promo zone (retailer-controlled, never brand-controlled).

Forbidden: stretching the lockup to fill an odd-proportioned strip; adding a retailer's promotional starburst or badge that overlaps the mark's clearspace.
03.2 — Wobblers & Hangers
Attention-getting shelf accessories
Core standard: small mark + one short benefit line only (e.g. "Tear. Fit. Done."), Deep Navy or Value Yellow background. Wobblers are a glance-read format — never carry the full lockup with wordmark plus supporting copy.

Minimum size: 60mm diameter/width — below this, mark-only with no copy.

Forbidden: die-cutting the wobbler into the shape of the tear-wave or block icon (creates an unapproved new mark variant); printing on both faces with different messages that contradict Part 1 tone rules.
03.3 — End-Cap & Gondola Displays
Larger-format retail environments
Core standard: primary lockup as header at maximum approved size for header width (same rule as PDQ header per Part 2.3) — this is a hero brand moment, treated identically to a PDQ header. Header background Deep Navy or Value Yellow only. Below the header, product facings follow standard packaging rules (Part 2) with no additional in-store-only graphics layered onto the packaging itself.

Approved variants: full end-cap header panel; gondola shelf-talker (smaller version of the same header, horizontal lockup, minimum 40mm).

Forbidden: introducing a display-specific color or graphic device not defined in Part 1's four-color system; co-branding a retailer's end-cap header without following Part 5's equal-weight/separation rules.
03.4 — Signage & Wayfinding
Aisle markers and category signage
Core standard: small mark only, paired with category language (e.g. "Cornice & Trim Systems") in Archivo — wayfinding signage is functional, not a brand hero moment, so the full lockup is never required.

Viewing distance: assumes 5-8m aisle-marker distance — small mark minimum 60mm, category text minimum 30mm height.

Global consideration: category language localizes per market; mark and minimum-size rules do not change.
Global Brand Standards · Part 4
Digital Ecosystem
Web, e-commerce, marketplaces, app icons, social, and the everyday corporate-digital surfaces (email, office docs) that carry the brand as often as packaging does. Parts 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 are fixed and inherited.
04.1 — Website & E-Commerce
Owned digital storefronts
Core standard: horizontal lockup in the site header, 32-48px height, left-aligned. Product tiles use the small mark as a corner badge, never the full lockup (competes with product photography). Hero sections may use the primary mark at large scale on Warm Paper or Deep Navy backgrounds only.

Checkout/e-commerce: small mark in footer/receipt contexts; trust badges ("Value Guarantee," "Patent Pending") permitted near checkout CTA per Part 2.4 placement logic, never inline with legal/payment icons.
04.2 — App Icons & Mobile
Icon-scale and in-app presence
Core standard: app icon = small mark, full-bleed, Navy background, per Part 1's favicon rule — no exceptions for platform-specific icon shapes (squircle/circle masks apply automatically at the OS level; the mark itself is never redrawn to pre-fit a shape). In-app: horizontal lockup in top nav only above 200px width; small mark in mobile nav/tab bars.
04.3 — Marketplace Listings
Amazon, retailer portals, third-party e-commerce
Core standard: primary product image follows Part 2 packaging front-panel rules exactly — marketplace thumbnails are a photograph of real packaging, not a redesigned digital-only layout. Storefront banner (where platform allows) uses the horizontal lockup per 04.1. A/B test imagery may vary lifestyle photography but never the mark, colors, or type treatment.

Forbidden: platform-specific badge stacking (marketplace's own badges, e.g. "Best Seller") overlapping the mark or its clearspace on the primary listing image.
04.4 — Social Presence
Avatars, covers, and post templates
Core standard: avatar = small mark centered on Deep Navy, per-platform safe-area cropping respected. Cover/banner images use the horizontal lockup at one edge (never centered, to survive platform UI overlays). Post templates: small mark as a consistent corner watermark on photography; full lockup reserved for announcement-style posts only.
04.5 — Email, Office & Corporate Documents
Signatures, templates, letterheads
Email signature: small mark inline, 20-24px, before name/title text — never the full lockup at signature scale.

Email templates (marketing/transactional): horizontal lockup in header per 04.1's website rule, same minimum sizes apply.

Letterhead: primary lockup top-left or centered per Part 1, Warm Paper background, footer carries legal entity name/registration in Archivo per Part 7 legal-copy minimums — never the mark repeated in a footer watermark.

Office templates (slides, one-pagers, invoices): title slide/cover uses primary lockup; running headers/footers on internal pages use small mark only, consistent corner position across the deck or document.
04.6 — Application Families Beyond Digital
How this guide scales to apparel, vehicles, events & more
Every touchpoint not explicitly named in this guide (apparel — caps, hoodies, hi-vis, workwear; vehicles — bakkies, vans, forklifts; events — gazebos, banners, trade-stand backdrops; tools/equipment; office/corporate beyond what's above) is governed by family, not by individual item:

Apparel & wearables: small mark only, embroidery/print per Part 7's one-color and low-fidelity rules — the primary lockup is never embroidered at garment scale.
Vehicles: horizontal lockup on doors/side panels sized to Part 1's large-format guidance (reviewed, not redrawn, above 400mm); small mark only on smaller vehicle surfaces (forklifts, trailers).
Events & environments: primary lockup on backdrops/media walls at hero scale (same treatment as PDQ/end-cap headers, Parts 2.3/3.3); small mark on functional structures (gazebo legs, stanchions).
Tools & field equipment: small mark only, one-color, per Part 7 stamp/emboss rules.

Any new application family not covered here follows the nearest analogous rule above (hero surface → primary lockup; small/functional surface → small mark) and should be escalated to governance (Part 8) if genuinely ambiguous, rather than improvised.
Global Brand Standards · Part 5
Co-Branding, Private Label & Licensing
Where brand systems usually get mangled in the wild. Parts 1 and 2 are fixed and inherited — this chapter governs only how the mark behaves next to other brands.
05.1 — Retailer / Licensee Lockup Rules
Two marks, one panel
Purpose: prevent Block Box from being visually subordinated or absorbed into a partner's identity while sharing space fairly.

Core standard: Block Box mark and any retailer/licensee logo are separated by a vertical rule, minimum separation = 1.5x Block Box's own clearspace unit (per Part 1). Both marks rendered at equal cap-height/visual weight — neither is scaled to dominate.

Approved variants: side-by-side horizontal lockup (retailer left, Block Box right, or per retailer's own brand-architecture convention) separated by the vertical rule; stacked variant for narrow panels, rule becomes horizontal, Block Box always in the lower position with equal weight maintained.

Minimum clearspace: each mark retains its own full clearspace per Part 1 in addition to the 1.5x separation rule — the two zones do not overlap or share margin.

Forbidden: merging the tear-wave into a retailer's logo or any shared graphic device; recoloring Block Box to match a retailer's palette; scaling Block Box smaller than the partner mark on any primary packaging or POS face; placing the two marks without the separating rule (implying a merged single brand).
05.2 — Private-Label Variants
When a retailer sells Block Box under its own name
Purpose: protect recognition of the underlying system even when the retailer's name leads.

Core standard: in private-label contexts, the retailer's brand may lead on the front panel, but a "Powered by Block Box" mark (small mark + wordmark at reduced but legible size, never below Part 1's minimum) is mandatory on the front or side panel — Block Box is never fully invisible to the end consumer.

Approved variant: "Powered by Block Box" set in Archivo Regular, small mark inline, placed in a lower corner or alongside the retailer's own regulatory copy zone.

Forbidden: private-label packaging with no Block Box attribution anywhere on the unit; recoloring or redrawing the small mark to match the retailer's palette; implying the retailer invented the tear-away system (no competing claim of originality in copy).

Global consideration: some markets require underlying-manufacturer disclosure by law — where local regulation is stricter than this standard, the stricter requirement governs placement, but the mark itself is never altered.
05.3 — Manufacturing-Partner Attribution
Crediting the licensed producer
Purpose: distinguish "made under license" (manufacturing partner) from "co-branded" (retail partner) relationships, which carry different rules.

Core standard: "Made under license" or "Manufactured under Block Box license" is required in Archivo, minimum 8pt/8px, adjacent to the mark on any packaging produced by a licensed manufacturer — this is attribution text, not a lockup, and does not receive its own clearspace zone.

Approved placement: back panel or regulatory zone alongside patent-pending notice; never on the front panel where it would compete with the primary brand moment.

Forbidden: a manufacturing partner's own logo appearing at equal or greater size than Block Box's mark on any consumer-facing panel; omitting the license attribution line entirely; abbreviating "Made under license" in ways that obscure the licensing relationship.

Global consideration: attribution line localizes by language per market but must retain the same legal meaning; where a jurisdiction requires additional licensee identification (company name, registration number), add it adjacent to — not in place of — this line.
Global Brand Standards · Part 6
Motion & Interactive
Motion expresses the fixed 10c identity — it never redesigns it. Parts 1-5 and 7 are fixed and inherited. Product photography/illustration direction: warehouse/installation contexts with the tear action as hero moment; illustration stays flat/geometric, never competing with real photography.
06.1 — Logo Animation Rules
Reveal, hold, exit
Purpose: let the mark move without ever looking redrawn or novelty-first.

Core standard: reveal = tear-wave draws on in a single continuous stroke (0.4-0.6s, ease-out), block fades/scales in beneath it at 95-100% scale (never overshoot past 100%). Hold minimum 1.5s at full lockup before any exit. Exit = simple fade or scale-to-98%, never a reverse-tear or "un-tearing" animation (implies the product re-sealing, which is false).

Approved easing: standard ease-out for entrances, ease-in-out for looping/idle states. Timing 0.3-0.6s per element — nothing snaps (0 duration) or lingers past 1s for a single reveal beat.

Prohibited: bounce/elastic/spring easing, fake 3D bevel or rotation animation, particle bursts, confetti, cartoon squash-and-stretch, spinning logo loops, drop-shadow pulsing, or any effect that makes the mark feel like a mascot rather than a mark.
06.2 — Video & Ad Title Cards
Openers, closers, supers, explainer frames
Core standard: opener = primary lockup per 06.1's reveal, Deep Navy or Warm Paper full-frame background, minimum 2s screen time. Closer/end board = primary lockup + one CTA line in Archivo, minimum 3s hold (accessibility floor, see 06.5). Supers (on-screen text over footage) use Archivo only, never Fredoka, with a solid or semi-opaque holding shape behind text for contrast.

Explainer/promo frames: product demonstration (the tear action) is the hero visual; mark appears as a corner watermark (small mark) during footage, full lockup reserved for opener/closer only.

Minimum hold times: any frame containing the primary lockup with wordmark: 2s minimum. Any frame with key claim text (e.g. "93% packaging cost reduction"): 3s minimum, per Part 7 legibility rule extended to time.
06.3 — Interactive & Demo Behavior
Web, app, kiosk, training flows
Core standard: hover/tap states use opacity or subtle scale (98-102%) only — no color inversion of the mark itself, no shape distortion. Animated diagrams (e.g. tear-force explainer) use the flat geometric illustration style from Part 6's inherited photography/illustration direction, never a literal video of the mark tearing itself.

Kiosk/demo screens & installer training: loop idle state = static lockup or slow ease-in-out breathing (98-100% scale, 3-4s cycle) — never continuous spinning or bouncing while unattended.

Marketplace/product video: follows 06.2's explainer frame rules exactly; no additional platform-specific motion graphics overlaid on the mark.
06.4 — Family Inheritance for Screens & Animated Signage
When apparel, vehicle, or event contexts include a screen
Core standard: per Part 4.6's family logic, any physical application family (event stand, vehicle, retail display) that incorporates an LED sign, digital display, or screen inherits this Part's motion rules exactly — a gazebo's LED header or a vehicle's digital sign follows 06.1/06.2 hold times and prohibited-behavior list, not a separate "signage motion" standard.
06.5 — Technical & Accessibility Constraints
Silent playback, low frame rate, reduced motion
Silent/autoplay-muted environments: all key claims and CTAs must be legible as on-screen text (supers per 06.2) — never conveyed by voiceover or sound effect alone.

Low-frame-rate/LED signage/low-spec devices: animation degrades gracefully to a simple cross-fade between static frames rather than attempting the full 06.1 reveal — motion is a progressive enhancement, not a requirement for brand recognition.

Reduced-motion accessibility: where a platform signals a reduced-motion preference, all animation drops to instant/near-instant fades (≤0.15s); no exceptions.

Minimum hold times (from 06.2) apply regardless of playback environment — a kiosk or LED sign on a fast loop still respects the 2-3s legibility floor per Part 7.
Global Brand Standards · Part 7
Global & Technical Constraints
Mandatory fallbacks for real-world failure conditions — poor substrate, ink gain, misregistration, low-DPI thermal print, overcopy, template crowding, icon-scale compression. Parts 1, 2 and 5 are fixed and inherited.
07.1 — Multilingual & RTL Layout
What translates, what never does
Never translated: the "Block Box" proper noun stays in Latin script in every market; mark geometry (rounded block, tear-wave) never mirrors, redraws, or adapts to local script conventions; "Value Guarantee" as a label device keeps its Latin wordmark with a localized translation set beside it, not replacing it.

Localizes freely: benefit copy, regulatory/compliance copy, support and documentation text, packaging descriptors (profile names may add local-language equivalents alongside the code, e.g. "NT108 · 110mm / Perfil 110mm").

RTL layout rule: in Arabic/Hebrew-style contexts, the surrounding text block and reading order mirror; the mark itself never mirrors or flips. In horizontal lockups, the mark's position relative to the text block flips to match reading direction (mark moves to the right edge when text flows right-to-left), but internal mark geometry stays fixed.

Font fallback for non-Latin scripts: per Part 1, headlines fall back to a rounded geometric sans of equivalent weight/x-height where Fredoka lacks coverage; body copy falls back to system sans in that script. This applies identically to RTL scripts — no italic or condensed substitutes.
07.2 — Low-Cost Print & Reduced-Fidelity Reproduction
Case-by-case fallback, not one combined note
One-color offset: solid Navy (preferred) or solid Yellow mark, no tints or gradients. Small mark only below 40mm.
Two-color offset: Navy + Yellow only, no Orange — tear-wave renders in whichever of the two provides 4.5:1 contrast against the fill.
Grayscale/B&W: 100% black on white or reversed white-on-black — never a mid-gray tint, which fails below 20mm print and at any thermal resolution.
Thermal label (shipping/warehouse): small mark only, solid black, minimum 15mm — the primary lockup is never attempted on thermal stock; wordmark is dropped, not shrunk.
Emboss / deboss / laser-etch: small mark only, tear-wave simplified to a single unbroken groove — no fill color implied, geometry must read by relief alone.
Stamp (rubber/ink stamp on corrugate): one-color small mark only, minimum 20mm — corrugate ink spread means anything smaller is not production-safe.
Corrugate direct print (low-DPI flexo): primary lockup permitted at ≥80mm only, with a printed white/yellow holding field beneath per Part 1 background rules; below that threshold, drop to small mark.
Low-resolution digital (icon compression, poor screens): small mark is the only version permitted below 32px; the wordmark is never attempted at low bitrate/compressed thumbnail sizes.
07.3 — Accessibility & Legibility Minimums
Contrast, size, and viewing-distance floors by context
Contrast: minimum 4.5:1 for all text and mark-on-background pairings (WCAG AA), no exceptions for decorative use.

Consumer packaging (front panel): assumes 0.3-1m handling distance; body/regulatory text never below 6pt/8px; benefit copy never below 10pt/13px.

Shelf strips / aisle signage: assumes 3-5m viewing distance; brand lockup never below 40mm height; no text element smaller than 20mm height.

Shipper cartons: assumes warehouse scanning distance (arm's length to 2m); small mark + codes only, minimum 15mm, no benefit copy required.

Mobile screens: assumes 25-40cm viewing distance; small mark minimum 24px per Part 1; body text minimum 14px equivalent; never rely on color alone to convey a state — pair with icon or text label.

Failure-condition rule: where ink gain, misregistration, or template crowding is expected (retailer-supplied packaging templates, third-party print runs), the smaller mandatory fallback version is used pre-emptively rather than risking the primary lockup degrading in production.
